# Realytica — product

Realytica is an AI project office that everyone on a building project can work in or connect to.

- It takes in every document, in English or any other language.
- It reads the latest laws and policy changes and works out the action items.
- It finds by itself where the project has issues, and suggests them.
- For any data or document it tells the people concerned what the information is and what their action item is.
- It shows analytics and action items at each level: portfolio, project, department, function and person.
- It answers with sources, fills an empty template or questionnaire from everything on the project, and gives monthly or quarterly reports.
- People work in it directly, integrate with it, or use connectors from the tools they already have.
- Eventually it is voice enabled.
- A named person still accepts what counts.

That is the definition. It is not all built: [What is built and what is not](#what-is-built-and-what-is-not) goes through it line by line, and the rest of this page describes what is there today.

The project it serves is a property through its whole life — land, design, approvals, construction, sales and handover, then running the finished building — and every profession that works on it. Engineering and due-diligence firms are the first users; developers, lawyers, valuers, chartered accountants, quantity surveyors, architects and site teams join the same project.

## How a project is organised

**Stages (when).** Four stages, each the state of the property: Land, Pre-construction, Under construction, Completed. The track of the four sits in the bar of every project page and shows no steps under them. Pressing a stage looks at the project in that stage, as it does in the example project: the selector lists the departments with a function at that stage, a department's tabs list those functions, and its pages name what is delivered then. Looking is free and changes nothing on the record: a person can go back and forth between stages as often as they like. The project is at one stage only, and that stage carries the word **Live** on the track whichever one is being looked at; the stage being looked at is in the colour of what is picked, and pressing the live stage goes back to it. The stage being looked at is in the address (`?stage=land`, `pre`, `build` or `done`, and nothing for the project's own stage), so it holds from page to page inside the project and through a reload, and is gone on leaving the project. What was filed, checked and decided while the project was at a stage is a section of Overview, shown for the stage being looked at. The finer steps are kept on the record, in that section, and that is where a project is moved from one step to the next.

| Stage | Steps kept on the record |
|---|---|
| Land | Opportunity · Feasibility · Acquisition |
| Pre-construction | Design · Approvals · Tender & procurement |
| Under construction | Mobilisation · Construction · Testing & commissioning · Completion (OC) |
| Completed | Handover · Operations (property management) |

A phased project can have each phase at its own step; the stage's record on Overview lists the phases standing at a step of their own.

**Departments (what kind of work).** Six departments, each a separate module that links to the others. A firm switches on its usual set; each project can override it.

| Department | Workstreams | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Finance & Investment | **Valuation** · Feasibility & underwriting · **Budget & cost to complete** · Funding & escrow · Tax | Live |
| Legal & Compliance | **Title & land records** · **Approvals & NOCs** · RERA · Contracts & disputes · Handover & society | Live |
| Engineering & Construction | **Progress & schedule** · **Technical due diligence** · **Site record** · Safety & environment | Live |
| Design & Architecture | Drawings & versions · Design compliance · RFIs · Coordination | Coming soon |
| Procurement & Supply Chain | BOQ & tenders · Purchase orders · Vendors · Deliveries | Coming soon |
| Commercial & Operations | Market & pricing · Sales & leasing inventory · Buyers & collections · Handover & defects · Property management | Coming soon |

Bold workstreams are built; the rest are listed with what they will produce.

The menu shows five departments, by one word each: Legal, Finance, Engineering, Commercial, Procurement. Design & Architecture is worked inside Engineering, as its **Design** function; the record underneath still keeps it as a department with its own people and roles. A workstream is called a function in the menu and carries one word on its tab (Title, Approvals, Valuation, Technical, Site and so on).

A project's departments are chosen when it is made and changed from **Overview › Departments on this project** (a workspace admin's control). Switching one off hides it and deletes nothing. A new project starts from the departments the firm's last project used.

**An engineering-only project.** A firm doing only the technical work runs Engineering & Construction alone. Its technical due diligence is one screen in five steps, each showing its own count: **Documents**, **Questions**, **Site**, **Observations**, **Report**. Four figures sit above them: documents in hand, checks answered, open findings and the cost to remedy. An observation carries its own cost to fix and when the money is needed; that figure is the remedy the cost table sums, so it is entered once. Documents are dropped straight onto the Documents step.

**The same steps in every department.** Finance and Legal run the steps on their own page: **Documents**, **Questions**, **Findings**, **Report**. Each has its own requirement sheet, its own questionnaires and its own findings with cost, and its own roles decide who may change them. Only Engineering has the Site step. The Report step creates that department's own report: the technical due diligence report in Engineering, a legal due diligence report in Legal (chain of title, requisitions and answers, findings, documents) and a financial due diligence report in Finance (indicative valuation, questions and answers, findings, documents).

**In the graph.** The graph is laid out the way the menu is: the four stages, the five departments by their one word, and under each department its functions, with Design as one function of Engineering. The finer steps are not drawn; each stage lists its steps, and the stage the project is in says which one it is at. A check, a document, an approval, a milestone, a site visit, a site entry and a questionnaire sit in the function that holds them, while that function's department is switched on. What carries a date sits in the stage the project was at on that date: a document once it is filed, a check once it is answered, a finding, a risk, a decision, a report, a site visit, a site entry, a questionnaire, a certified report and an engagement. Not every record has both. A finding, a risk, a decision and a report are placed in a stage and in no function; an approval and a milestone are placed in a function and in no stage; an action is placed in neither and is reached from the finding, risk or check that calls for it. Nothing is left unconnected: a record that nothing else places, such as an action with nothing behind it, is joined to the project itself, so a walk that starts at the project reaches everything on the file. Being cited in the chat does not count as being placed. A questionnaire is a node in its department's work, and every answered question is a node joined to the documents and photographs that prove it; an answer a model suggested is marked until a person confirms it. On the Graph page a selected record lists what it touches in plain words, grouped by the kind of thing at the other end, with the documents and the site record first. The words follow the state of the record: a check rests on a deed that is on file and still needs one that is not, and an approval is needed before the work it gates whether or not it is in hand. A function is named with its department (Legal › Handover), because two functions share a word.

**One short menu.** A project has one bar, the app's own top bar. On the left, after the project's name, one selector says where you are: Overview, a department, or a place the whole project shares (Documents, Review, Registers, Reports, People, Graph; Review and People are for the firm's own people). On the right is the track of the four stages, then three plain icons: alerts, the command palette and focus. Nothing else lives there. The way back to the portfolio is the sidebar; the health of the file is the first thing on Overview; what waits for a decision is a blue dot on the selector and the first row under the alerts bell, which walks through it. Inside a department the row under the bar is its functions at the stage being looked at, one tab each, sharing the row equally, with **Summary** first for what belongs to the department as a whole; a function that is not built yet is listed in a quieter ink. The tabs carry no counts: a blue dot marks a function with something waiting for a person (today on Valuation and Site, the two whose waiting list is kept by page). Documents, checks, findings, risks and actions, and reports are worked on inside each department; Overview has one row of links to the same lists across the whole project.

**What a stage shows.** A function shows at the stages it has work in. They are the example project's, function for function, and a test holds the two lists together: Title, Approvals and Site in all four, RERA from Pre-construction on, Safety only while the building goes up, Procurement's four only in the two middle stages. So Legal, Finance, Engineering and Commercial are in the menu at every stage, and Procurement at two. What a function hands over at a stage is narrower than the work it has there: Title is worked on in all four stages and delivers a title opinion at Land and the conveyance at Completed. A department's Summary names a function's deliverable for the stage being looked at ("Delivers at Land: …") where it has one, and says nothing where it has none. The selector lists a department while one of its functions shows, and a department's Summary lists the same functions as its tabs. Design shows inside Engineering while it is switched on, at the stages its four workstreams have work in. At the stage the project stands at, a function that already holds the project's records also shows, whatever its stages: an answered check, a document in hand, an approval, a milestone, a site visit, a site entry, a questionnaire, a certified report, or an estimate with enough on file to say something. Work already done is never missing from the screen a person lands on. When a stage is pressed, the page on screen stays if it shows at that stage. A function that does not show there gives way to its department's Summary, and a department with nothing there to Overview. Overview and the shared places stay where they are. A link that names a function, from the chat, an alert or a card, always opens it: at the stage being looked at when the function shows there, otherwise at the project's own stage, otherwise at the first stage it has work in. The records inside a page are not yet narrowed to the stage.

**A function is one page.** A built function (Title, Approvals, Progress, Budget) is one scrolling page with a rail of its parts down the left: the estimate beside the certified report, the work that is its own (the chain of title, the approvals register, the progress board, the cost report), its checks, its documents and its connections. The rail is a column of icons that opens to show the names on hover; on a phone it lies across the top.

**Finance › Budget.** The part of the page that is Budget's own is the **Cost Report**. A Cost Report is started for a month and is a draft until it is issued. Its package ledger has one row for each work package, with four figures: the budget, the purchase orders issued, the amount paid, and what the package is now expected to cost when it is finished. Two sheets read the same rows and are never typed: budget against PO issued against amount paid, and budget against anticipated cost with the variance. A new report starts from the work packages the project already keeps, each with its budget. The ledger is typed row by row, pasted as a table, or taken from a CSV or Excel file that has a column for the package and at least one of the four figures; a row is matched to one already there by its code or its name. A row's name, code and budget are kept the same on the project's work package, so the graph draws it. Under the ledger are a variation log (what moved the cost, by how much, and whether it was drawn from contingency), basic price adjustments (tender rate against current rate, with the difference where a quantity is given), the photographs chosen for the period, and a note. Above the ledger, **Since last report** reads the report against the last one issued for an earlier period: the total forecast then and now, the three packages that moved most with what accounts for each (a variation logged against the package, or a package awarded since), whatever no variation or award accounts for as its own unexplained amount, the running bills dated in between as claimed against certified, and the decisions needed (packages forecast over budget, packages with no PO). Whoever fills the ledger can type one line of reason against a package's change. Issuing freezes the report under the name of whoever issued it, and it prints as a document of its own, a cover and the sheets; to change a figure it is reopened. A lead, contributor or signer in Finance fills the ledger; issuing and reopening are for the lead or signer.

Below the Cost Report is the register of contracts and running bills: a contract with a contractor against work packages, a bill under it with its lines, an amount passed line by line, a certificate issued on the bill under a signer's name with its deductions, and the payments recorded against it. Issuing or withdrawing a certificate, voiding a payment and removing a bill or a contract are for the lead or signer. What is certified counts only under a certificate that stands.

What Budget does not do yet: every figure on a Cost Report is entered, not worked out from the bills; nothing reads a bill's own file into the register; no alert is raised from either; and the chat and the status report do not read a Cost Report's figures (the chat reaches the work packages, contracts, bills and certificates through the graph). An outside collaborator sees none of it unless the budget is ticked on their grant.

**The site map.** The map on Site is drawn from the state's own published land map, read by survey number, and a site that stands on many survey numbers is read for each. Under the place picker is one line for every number the file knows: the project's own, each one a document was read as stating (with the document, and whether a person has accepted that reading or it still waits), and any a person types, several at once. What a person typed comes first; the rest are in the order of the numbers, the parts of one survey number together. A zero before a part is the same number, so 77/03 and 77/3 are one line naming both papers; it is asked of the map as 77/3, and again as the paper writes it where a state's map does not hold it so. A number that looks like another the papers state with its stroke lost in reading, 472 beside 47/2 where no whole number in the papers is that long, is said to be a likely misreading while nobody has accepted that reading, and waits unticked; a person can still have it read, and a number a person has accepted is a number like any other. An accepted number is ticked; one that waits can be ticked too, and then says it was read by machine and not yet accepted. Each ticked number is read on a request of its own, in turn, and its line says where it stands: reading, read with its area, not in the published map with the numbers that start the same way, or failed with the reason. One failure does not stop the rest, though a run ends when the map fails to answer three numbers running, and waits when the server asks it to; a number already read is read again only when asked. A range, or two numbers run together, is shown as it was written and not read, with how to write it; a dash from a lower number to a higher is read as a range, and the line says how to write a part of a number. Every read is kept, one for each parcel, and can be removed singly or all together. The first is kept whole and each one after it keeps the outlines of the tanks and streams round it once for the site, so a parcel more weighs under a fifth of what the first does. A read that would make the project's record too heavy to open is not kept, a parcel read again among them where the fresh read is heavier: its line says how many parcels are kept and why, and the run stops there. The map frames all the parcels, draws each outline with its survey number on it, and draws the state's water, drains and planning zones round them once; a finding about one parcel names its number. Under the map each parcel's area from its outline is listed beside the extent the register records for it, with the total. What the documents were accepted as stating is set beside the map like for like: the extents of documents about different survey numbers are added up, they are set against the parcels for those numbers and no others, and only once every one of those numbers is read; until then it says how many are. An extent whose document names no survey number is taken as the whole site's, and set against every parcel read once every number the file states is among them; where several such documents do not agree, none is taken for the site. A deed read off the page as naming one number may be for more: where its figure is more than that parcel holds, nothing is set against it until every number the file states is read, and where it is then the figure of every parcel read it is set against them all. Nothing is set against a document that states a range, or a number two parcels read both carry, and it says why. It is two figures and the difference between them, raised when they are a twentieth apart or more; the lender's check of the papers on Value counts the same land the same way, so deeds for different survey numbers are added up there too, not set against each other. The valuation is offered that total as one candidate for the land area, last in rank, and one guidance value, with the parcel it was read for; where the parcels carry different values the guideline value of the plot counts each at its own, and none is given for a site when one of its parcels carries no published value, whether the others agree or not: which parcel that is, is said. An outline from the state's map is a government record read by machine: not a survey, and not evidence until a person attaches the extract. Karnataka's map holds whole survey numbers, so 41/1 and 41/2 are both answered by the parcel for 41, which is counted once and says it is the whole number wherever it is shown; more land on the map than a document, the land area on the project or a person's own outline gives for a part of a number is said, not raised, up to that parcel's own area. Two parcels with one number in two villages are told apart by their villages.

**The example project.** *Lakeview Tower* is a whole project with made-up data, open to anybody at `/example` without signing in and reached from the landing page and the portfolio. It shows all twenty-four functions of the five departments at each of the four stages, each as its own page: the papers it expects, its values with the page each was read from, tables, photographs, maps with water, drains and their buffers, town-planning zones, roads, the power line and the airport zone, what a proper project expects of it, its flags, the copilot's insights, and the law or standard it rests on. A value the copilot read is accepted or rejected on the field or in the proof pane beside it; a paper opens in a pop-up; a paper or a map has one home and is referred to from the other functions that use it. Nothing in it is saved, and its copilot's replies are scripted. A function or department that is not built yet links from a real project to its page there.

**Colour.** Teal marks what is picked or can be followed; blue marks what the copilot wrote or suggested and no person has accepted yet; green, amber and red are kept for status.

- **Observations and mitigations** (step 4) is the table the report is read for: area by area, what was seen, its risk category, the mitigation, the code it is judged against, and the photographs that show it. An observation is a finding — the same record a check raises or the chat proposes — so nothing is entered twice. Below the table sit the photographs nothing cites yet, from the document register and from the phone's site log, each with what a model saw in it and any finding it suggested; one press starts an observation from a photograph or attaches it to one. A site-log photograph is filed onto the register the first time it is used.
- **Photographs** (step 3) is a contact sheet of every photograph on the project, from the document register and the phone's site log. Each has a caption and an area to fill in, a description, and one switch for whether it prints in the report. A photograph an observation cites is in the report with that observation, and says so. The description works the way a value read off a document does: a model's reading (what is visible, never a cause or a severity) arrives on the photograph as a suggestion, a person accepts it or edits it, and only then is it the photograph's description and printed in the report. A person can also write it without asking a model.
- **What prints is chosen where the work is.** An observation, a photograph and a question each carry an *In report* switch; switching one off keeps it on the file and leaves it out of the report. Whole sections are moved or removed in the report itself.
- **Report** (step 5) creates the **technical due diligence report** and opens it. It starts with the sections an engineer's report has — the property, scope and basis, building information, observations and mitigations, remedial cost, the inspection record, documents reviewed and outstanding, limitations, opinion — and three of them are tables that read the steps before: the answered questionnaire (a suggestion nobody confirmed is printed as not answered, and answers resting on the seller's word alone are counted), the observations with their risk, mitigation and reference, and the document sheet with what was not received. The photographs each observation cites print under the table, captioned with the row, the date and the coordinates. An **at a glance** section counts the observations by risk category, with a bar for each. A **site photographs** section prints the photographs chosen for the report that no observation already shows. The tables print the same on screen, on paper and in the Word file, and an issued report keeps them as they stood. The three tables also export on their own.

The pieces under the other steps:

- **The requirement sheet** (step 1): every document the checks expect, by discipline — Architecture, Structural, MEP, Statutory — each one not asked, asked for or in hand. Tick the ones to chase, name who is asked and by when, and a tracked request goes out for each. A line turns to in hand on its own when the paper is filed. It copies as a list and exports as a spreadsheet.
- **The checklist** (step 1, under the requirement sheet): a list of papers somebody outside sent, loaded as it was written from pasted text, a CSV or TSV, or an Excel workbook. A row that names the columns (item, stage, package, owed by) is used where there is one, and a heading sets the stage or the package for the lines under it. Loading the same list again adds nothing. Lines are added, renamed, moved and removed one at a time. The list is shown by stage, then package, with how many are in hand of each. A line is in hand when a filed document carries its name or a person marks it, with the document where they name one. A document that only looks like it is offered under the line and a person confirms or refuses it. One press sends one tracked request to each party for everything it still owes, and the request closes when all of it is in hand. A list belongs to the department whose desk it was loaded on, and is changed by the firm's people who may edit there. The chat does not read it yet, and it is not in the graph or the reports.
- **The charts** under Observations: findings and checks by discipline, and what the remedies cost by how soon they are needed.
- **Supporting documents**: the client's deeds, approvals and valuation still arrive. They are read and citable, and show on the department page as supporting documents; one can be given to a workstream here. Switching Legal or Finance on later puts each where it belongs.

- **The questionnaire** (step 2): the client's own list of questions about the building, imported as written from Excel, Word, PDF, a spreadsheet saved as CSV, plain text, or pasted text. One dropped in the chat is taken in the same way. Answers already in the sheet come in as the seller's. Each answer records where it came from — the seller said so, a document states it, it was seen on site, or it is the engineer's view — and the documents or photographs that stand behind it, with the page. The chat can answer from the documents on file; its answers arrive as suggestions and wait for a person to confirm them, one by one or all at once. The answered sheet copies as text and exports as Excel or PDF, in the order the questions came in, each answer with where it came from and the paper and page behind it.

The chat does all of this too: it reads the requirement sheet, the questionnaire and the technical summary, and proposes asking for documents, moving a document, or changing the departments (the last for an admin to approve).

**Workstreams (the work inside).** Each workstream holds, stage by stage:

- a **quick assessment** — a living estimate, built from the project's own findings and data with AI help, following the official method for that kind of assessment, re-read whenever anything changes;
- **certified reports** from named professionals — the figure of record;
- its **checks** and **records** (approvals, milestones, site entries, comparables…);
- the **documents** it owns, from the one shared vault.

**Engagements (who it's for).** A piece of work a client commissions — acquisition screening, title due diligence, technical due diligence, a lender's independent engineer, an indicative valuation. An engagement draws on workstreams and produces one deliverable. Checks live once, in their workstream, so the same title check serves every engagement that needs it.

## Quick assessments and certified reports

A quick assessment fills its inputs in this order, and a higher source always overrides a lower one:

1. Verified data on the file
2. The project's own documents
3. Government records, laws and policies (registered sales, guidance values)
4. Regulatory and professional standards (IBBI, RICS, IS codes, NBC)
5. Licensed transaction data
6. Portal listings (asking prices)
7. Reputable published sources
8. Standard assumptions

Every input shows its source. Assumptions are labelled; a figure resting more than half on them shows only as a rough range. Two sources at the same level that disagree are shown side by side for a person to pick.

A certified report — a title opinion, a valuation, a progress certificate — is uploaded by its signer and read for its figures, signer, registration, date and scope. It becomes the figure of record. The quick assessment keeps running beside it, and the report is flagged **for revisiting** when later evidence moves the estimate:

- a figure more than 10% from the certified one;
- progress more than 5 points from the certified progress;
- any new blocker, or a new condition where the report was clear.

The flag goes to the department's lead and the professional who signed.

## People and access

Roles per department: **Lead** (runs it, accepts proposals, owns deadlines), **Contributor** (adds documents, records, site entries), **Signer** (certifies its reports; their registration is recorded), **Viewer**. A person can hold different roles in different departments. Firm roles set the default: owners and managers lead every department, staff contribute, viewers read. The project's team list says otherwise where it needs to, and outside collaborators reach only the departments they are given.

**Who accepts what was read.** Only a lead or a signer of the department a value belongs to may accept it, correct it, set it aside or reopen it, pick one of two readings that disagree, or move the document out of the function that holds it. A contributor files documents and records what they find, and decides nothing that was read. The server holds every way in to this rule: the document's own row, a card on a check, the Value tab, an approval typed or pressed in the chat, a step of a plan. A page route answers 403 and names the department; the chat takes what the person may decide and says what it left, and for which department. Staff are contributors by default, so they are made a lead or a signer on the project's team list before they accept. What the rule leaves open is listed in [auth.md](auth.md#who-decides-what-a-document-states): a value typed onto a check by hand, a finding or a suggested answer accepted from a card, marking a row rejected, a comparable added by hand, and undo.

## One chat, and what it may do

One chat on every project, aware of the page you are on. The chat and the links between departments **propose**; a person **approves** where the proposal lands. Anything that leaves the system — an email, an invoice, a filing, money — needs a named approver. Internal alerts fire on their own.

**It goes where you say.** A department, a function, a shared place or a stage named in a sentence opens: "open Approvals", "show Legal at Land", "the checks for Approvals", "back to the live stage". No model is needed, and the reply is one line saying where it went, the stage when that changed, and the figure that matters there. A name two pages share ("Handover" is Legal's and Commercial's) is asked back as a choice, and a department that is switched off says so. A short name nothing has is said to be unknown, and nothing moves; a longer sentence that holds a shared place's word among others still opens that place. A name in quotes is a record's first, and a page's only when no record has it. Asking to see a stage never moves the project to it; only a sentence that says the project has moved, or is to be moved, does. A question carried in a link waits in the message box for the person to send.

**Links land where the record lives.** A record named in an answer, a citation or a chip under a reply opens the page of the function that holds it, at its part of that page. A document, a check, an approval, a milestone and a site entry are marked there; an estimate and a certified report open their part of the page. A record no function's page holds opens in its register. Where the department is switched off, a document or a check opens in its register, a certified report opens as the paper it is, and anything else opens in the graph. A stage pressed in an answer changes the stage in view as pressing the stage track does, and a check or a document that is open stays open.

**It answers about the page.** On a function's page, "what's missing", "summarise" and "which findings are critical" are answered for that function and say so ("On Title: …"); asking about the whole project widens the answer. The questions offered under the box are the page's own on the built functions, each department's Summary and the shared registers; anywhere else they follow from where the file stands. Moving between pages keeps the thread, and a question shows where it was asked when that is not the page in view. An outside collaborator is told a function's or a department's page opened without its figure, and an answer about a page opens with "Of what you have been given" and leaves out the firm's estimate.

**Earlier chats.** One control in the chat's header lists the chats on the project, each by its first question, its day and the page it started on, with a search once there are more than eight. A chat that opens with papers dropped in is listed by what was read. An earlier chat opens to read, and can be continued or renamed. The last line of the list lets the firm's own people delete every chat on the project, for everyone, after being asked once.

**A document dropped in.** The reply says where each paper was filed: the function and the stage, or Documents when no function holds it. For a paper no function holds, the likely functions are offered and none is picked; pressing one files the paper there at once, and it can be undone. An outside collaborator is offered none, because giving a paper to a function is for the firm's own people. The canvas opens the function's documents with the new row marked. The reply then says how many of the paper's values go to which function's checks, and calls out a value that differs from one on a check, on the project record or on another accepted paper, with both values and where each came from. Two areas or lengths within a unit and half a percent of each other are one measure rounded twice, and any other number has to be the same number. A paper for one parcel of a site does not differ from the site, and a value a person has already decided for a paper is not raised again for that paper. When the paper brings a value, a flag or a difference, the firm's own people are also told what it reaches downstream, including a certified report that rests on it. Nothing a paper states is on the file until a person accepts it, and a differing value never overwrites the one recorded. One chip opens the paper in the graph.

**Approving in a sentence.** Typed words accept in three ways, and only as the whole sentence: "approve all" (or "accept everything", "approve both", "approve them") takes what the last reply of that chat raised and the values on the papers it filed; "approve all open" (or "approve everything waiting") takes every card open on the project, for the firm's own people; and "approve", "accept", "skip", "reject" or "set aside" with a card's exact title in quotes takes or sets aside that one card, when the last reply listed it. A bare "yes", "ok" or "skip", a card's name without quotes, a question, and a sentence with a no or a later in it take nothing; where the last reply left something waiting, the reply says what waits and where, and offers it as choices to press. A choice carries the cards and papers it means, so pressing one acts on exactly those, only while they still wait, and it is a button only under the last reply of the chat on screen.

**It answers with its sources.** A citation sits in the sentence it supports, as a chip that carries the paper's name and the page. Pressing it opens the paper at that page. The paper opens over the work surface and leaves the conversation beside it; Escape closes it and returns to where the person was. A citation to a record that is not on the file stays on screen and opens nothing, so a claim never looks sourced when it is not. The record is asked before any page: a value the project already holds (the city, the address, the assessment year) is said from the record, and under an answer given by rule the chat says where the facts it is about stand, each as approved by a named person or still waiting. "What do we know about…", "what was decided", "what is still undecided" and "what changed" are answered from the project's memory, with no model. The detail is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#the-projects-memory).

**What goes into it.** Files reach the chat by the paperclip, the camera, the microphone, a paste, or a drop anywhere on the chat's column. A paper is filed as above. A list of questions somebody sent is taken in as a questionnaire, with answers suggested from the file; its chip opens the Questions step at that sheet. The notes of a meeting are kept as a meeting, and what they decided, what is to be done and what was left open wait on cards. A voice note is put into words where a transcription model is set up, and becomes a proposed entry on the site log, each line beside the words it came from. A photograph of the site is filed to Progress. A file keeps its name whatever script the name is written in.

**A job with many steps is shown first.** An instruction that comes to several steps, or to one step over more than ten records, is laid out as a plan with what each step will touch. Nothing starts until a person runs it; it can be stopped and taken up again; and what its steps raise still waits for a person where it waited before.

**Every change can be undone.** A reply that changed the record says what it changed, and its author can undo that message on its own, later, without taking back anything done since.

**Reports and letters from a sentence.** "Write this week's status for the owner" drafts a status report for the period named: what changed, what waits and on whom, and what comes next, every line with the record behind it. It is made by code from the record, so no model adds a line. A letter, a reply to a paper, a request for information and the minutes of a meeting are drafted the same way, each statement marked with its source, and are approved by a lead or signer by name before they are exported as a Word file. The app sends nothing itself.

## Links between departments

Live now: title and approvals feed the valuation's drivers and lender checks; approvals gate construction (work logged without a commencement certificate is flagged); progress feeds the as-is value and the cost to complete. Procurement ⇄ Finance, Legal ⇄ Design, Design ⇄ Construction, Construction ⇄ Commercial and Commercial ⇄ Finance arrive with their departments.

## Alerts

In the web app, the site app and by email: approvals expiring or lapsed, certified reports to revisit, work logged before it is allowed, milestones behind, likely to finish late or late, an extension of time that looks likely, serious issues from site, and an action past its date. An approval that is simply not on file yet is an alert in the app and is not mailed.

**Before a date is missed.** The schedule is brought in on the progress board from where it is kept: a pasted table, a CSV or an Excel file with an activity, a planned finish and, where the sheet has them, a planned start, a weight and a percent complete. Rows that could not be read are listed back with the reason. Bringing it in again moves the dates of the milestones it names, keeps the progress recorded for them, and keeps the first planned finish so a re-plan shows. From 14 days before its planned finish a milestone is behind when its progress is under the straight line between its planned start and finish, or under 80% where no start is known. With progress recorded on two days or more, a finish is projected at the current rate and flagged when it lands after the planned one; the project is likely to need an extension of time when its projected completion is after its planned completion, or that date has passed with work unfinished. One alert stands for a milestone's warning until its date, and its words follow the days left. The projection is a straight line through what was recorded, not a programme: it knows nothing of sequence, float or resources.

Email and push go to the lead and the signer of the department the alert belongs to, and an overdue action also to the person it is on. Nobody is sent what they could not read in the app. Alerts are worked out when a project is saved, and once a day for every project by a timer (01:30 UTC, 07:00 in India), so a date that comes up on a day nobody touches the project raises its alert that morning. The timer runs on the production deployment, and only where its secret is set. A new document or a new value raises no alert of its own.

## The site app

A native app for Android and iOS, for site work only: the daily site log (manpower, work done, progress against milestones, weather), geo-tagged photos, issues and snags. It works with no signal and syncs when it can. It signs in by scanning a pairing code from the web app.

## Integrations

Read-only public lookups: the state's published land map by survey number, published guidance values, map layers (water, drains, planning zones) and places. Portal listings for comparables are searched where a scraping service is configured. Land records, encumbrance certificates and RERA registration are not fetched: the registries have no interface a machine may use, so the app names the portal and how to get the paper, and reads the paper once it is filed.

Out of the app go files: PDF, Word, Excel and CSV. Nothing is written to government portals or banks. There is no connector to a drive, a mailbox or a messaging app yet, and no API key or webhook for another system; see the table below.

## What is built and what is not

The definition at the top of this page, line by line, against the code as it stands. **Built** means it works today. **Partly** names the part that does. **Not yet** means it is not in the code.

|---|---|---|---|
| a | Takes in every document, in English or any other language | Partly | Every file is kept and filed, and keeps its name in any script. With no model, the text layer and the bundled OCR read English and Kannada print, and the rules that type a document and lift its values are written for English. With a model configured, a page in another script is read by the model: a name or a place is kept as written beside its English reading, and a survey or document number is copied and never spelt out in English. That has been measured on made-up Kannada, Telugu and Devanagari pages only (`pnpm eval:multilingual`). The screens are in English. |

A model suggests a finding from a photograph, and the chat proposes findings, risks and actions as cards when asked. A daily timer brings the alerts of every project up to date (approvals expiring or lapsed, reports to revisit, work logged before it is allowed, milestones, serious site issues, actions past their date), so those are found on a day nobody opens the project. | d | Tells the people concerned what a new document or value is, and what their action item is | Partly | Alerts for a fixed list of conditions go to the department's lead and signer, and an overdue action to the person it is on: in the app, by email where a mail key is set, and by push to paired phones. A new document or value by itself notifies nobody, and no message names each person's action item. |
| e | Analytics and action items at each level | Partly | Each level has its page: Portfolio (stage, health and alerts for each project, decisions waiting, requests out, the next fortnight), a project's Overview, a department's Summary, a function's page with its estimate, and My work for a person. | g | Fills an empty template or questionnaire from everything on the project | Partly | A questionnaire is taken in from Excel, Word, PDF, CSV or pasted text, answers are suggested from the file with the paper and page behind each, a person confirms them, and it goes out as Excel or PDF. All are made when a person asks. Nothing is produced on a schedule, a quarter is not a period the status report knows by name, and a Cost Report covers a month. |
