# Realytica — product

Realytica is an AI project office that everyone on a building project can work in or connect to.

- It takes in every document, in English or any other language.
- It reads the latest laws and policy changes and works out the action items.
- It finds by itself where the project has issues, and suggests them.
- For any data or document it tells the people concerned what the information is and what their action item is.
- It shows analytics and action items at each level: portfolio, project, department, function and person.
- It answers with sources, fills an empty template or questionnaire from everything on the project, and gives monthly or quarterly reports.
- People work in it directly, integrate with it, or use connectors from the tools they already have.
- Eventually it is voice enabled.
- A named person still accepts what counts.

That is the definition. It is not all built: [What is built and what is not](#what-is-built-and-what-is-not) goes through it line by line, and the rest of this page describes what is there today.

The project it serves is a property through its whole life — land, design, approvals, construction, sales and handover, then running the finished building — and every profession that works on it. Engineering and due-diligence firms are the first users; developers, lawyers, valuers, chartered accountants, quantity surveyors, architects and site teams join the same project.

## How a project is organised

**Stages (when).** Four stages, each the state of the property: Land, Pre-construction, Under construction, Completed. The track of the four sits in the bar of every project page and shows no steps under them. Pressing a stage looks at the project in that stage, as it does in the example project: the selector lists the departments with a function at that stage, a department's tabs list those functions, and its pages name what is delivered then. Looking is free and changes nothing on the record: a person can go back and forth between stages as often as they like. The project is at one stage only, and that stage carries the word **Live** on the track whichever one is being looked at; the stage being looked at is in the colour of what is picked, and pressing the live stage goes back to it. The stage being looked at is in the address (`?stage=land`, `pre`, `build` or `done`, and nothing for the project's own stage), so it holds from page to page inside the project and through a reload, and is gone on leaving the project. What was filed, checked and decided while the project was at a stage is a section of Overview, shown for the stage being looked at. The finer steps are kept on the record, in that section, and that is where a project is moved from one step to the next.

| Stage | Steps kept on the record |
|---|---|
| Land | Opportunity · Feasibility · Acquisition |
| Pre-construction | Design · Approvals · Tender & procurement |
| Under construction | Mobilisation · Construction · Testing & commissioning · Completion (OC) |
| Completed | Handover · Operations (property management) |

A phased project can have each phase at its own step; the stage's record on Overview lists the phases standing at a step of their own.

**Departments (what kind of work).** Six departments, each a separate module that links to the others. A firm switches on its usual set; each project can override it.

| Department | Functions | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Finance & Investment | **Valuation** · Feasibility & underwriting · **Budget & cost to complete** · Funding & escrow · Tax · Lenders · Ledgers | Live |
| Legal & Compliance | **Title & land records** · **Approvals & NOCs** · RERA · Contracts · Handover & society · Litigation | Live |
| Engineering & Construction | **Progress & schedule** · **Technical due diligence** · **Site record** · Safety & environment | Live |
| Design & Architecture | Twenty-two functions under four professions: Architect, Interiors and landscape, Structural engineer, Services engineer | Field sheets only |
| Procurement & Supply Chain | **BOQ & tenders** · Purchase orders · Vendors · Deliveries | Tenders is live; the rest are field sheets only |
| Commercial & Operations | Market & pricing · Marketing & launch · Sales & leasing inventory · Buyers & collections · Handover & defects · Property management | Field sheets only; Property management has none |

Bold functions are built in full: a quick assessment, checks, their own registers and a certified report. Every other function but one is a page of **field sheets and documents** and nothing more: no quick assessment, no checks of its own, no certified report. Property management keeps no field sheets. Lenders, Ledgers, Litigation, Marketing and eighteen of Design's functions are new. [departments.md](departments.md) lists every function with its sheets, records, situations and reports.

The menu shows five departments, by one word each: Legal, Finance, Engineering, Commercial, Procurement. Design & Architecture is worked inside Engineering, as its **Design** function; the record underneath still keeps it as a department with its own people and roles. A workstream is called a function in the menu and carries one word on its tab (Title, Approvals, Valuation, Technical, Site and so on).

A project's departments are chosen when it is made and changed from **Overview › Departments on this project** (a workspace admin's control). Switching one off hides it and deletes nothing. A new project starts from the departments the firm's last project used.

**An engineering-only project.** A firm doing only the technical work runs Engineering & Construction alone. Its technical due diligence is one screen in five steps, each showing its own count: **Documents**, **Questions**, **Site**, **Observations**, **Report**. Four figures sit above them: documents in hand, checks answered, open findings and the cost to remedy. An observation carries its own cost to fix and when the money is needed; that figure is the remedy the cost table sums, so it is entered once. Documents are dropped straight onto the Documents step.

**The same steps in every department.** Finance and Legal run the steps on their own page: **Documents**, **Questions**, **Findings**, **Report**. Each has its own papers owed, its own questionnaires and its own findings with cost, and its own roles decide who may change them. Only Engineering has the Site step. The Report step creates that department's own report: the technical due diligence report in Engineering, a legal due diligence report in Legal (chain of title, requisitions and answers, findings, documents) and a financial due diligence report in Finance (indicative valuation, questions and answers, findings, documents).

**In the graph.** The graph is laid out the way the menu is: the four stages, the five departments by their one word, and under each department its functions, with Design as one function of Engineering. The finer steps are not drawn; each stage lists its steps, and the stage the project is in says which one it is at. A check, a document, an approval, a milestone, a site visit, a site entry and a questionnaire sit in the function that holds them, while that function's department is switched on. What carries a date sits in the stage the project was at on that date: a document once it is filed, a check once it is answered, a finding, a risk, a decision, a report, a site visit, a site entry, a questionnaire, a certified report and an engagement. Not every record has both. A finding, a risk, a decision and a report are placed in a stage and in no function; an approval and a milestone are placed in a function and in no stage; an action is placed in neither and is reached from the finding, risk or check that calls for it. Nothing is left unconnected: a record that nothing else places, such as an action with nothing behind it, is joined to the project itself, so a walk that starts at the project reaches everything on the file. Being cited in the chat does not count as being placed. A questionnaire is a node in its department's work, and every answered question is a node joined to the documents and photographs that prove it; an answer a model suggested is marked until a person confirms it. On the Graph page a selected record lists what it touches in plain words, grouped by the kind of thing at the other end, with the documents and the site record first. The words follow the state of the record: a check rests on a deed that is on file and still needs one that is not, and an approval is needed before the work it gates whether or not it is in hand. A function is named with its department (Legal › Handover), because two functions share a word.

**One short menu.** A project has one bar, the app's own top bar. On the left, after the project's name, one selector says where you are: Overview, a department, or a place the whole project shares (Documents, Reports, People, Graph; People is for the firm's own people, and Review and the registers are reached from Overview and by name). On the right is the track of the four stages, then three plain icons: alerts, the command palette and focus. Nothing else lives there. The way back to the portfolio is the sidebar; what needs a person, what is slipping, what is owed and what changed in cost are the first four lines of Overview; what waits for a decision is a blue dot on the selector and the first row under the alerts bell, which walks through it. Inside a department the row under the bar is its functions at the stage being looked at, one tab each, sharing the row equally, with **Summary** first for what belongs to the department as a whole; a function that is not built yet is listed in a quieter ink. The tabs carry no counts: a blue dot marks a function with something waiting for a person (today on Valuation and Site, the two whose waiting list is kept by page). Documents, checks, findings, risks and actions, and reports are worked on inside each department; Overview has one row of links to the same lists across the whole project.

**Overview.** It opens with four lines, each a question a person running the project asks first: **Needs you** (what waits to be accepted, replies to confirm, decisions to make, actions past their date, and what the daily review suggested), **Slipping** (milestones late, behind or late at the current rate, and where completion lands against the plan), **Papers owed** (how many, how many overdue or not asked, and the parties that owe the most) and **Cost** (the forecast against the budget on the last Cost Report issued, the movement since the one before, and the packages over budget). Each part of a line opens the page that holds it, and a line with nothing to act on says so in a sentence. Papers are owed to a department, so a count or a party on the Papers owed line opens the Documents of the department holding most of those papers, the line's arrow opens the department holding the most owed, and a reply to confirm opens the department whose list it waits on; a request made by hand, which belongs to no department, opens the sidebar's Requests. Under them are the five figures of where the file stands, the links to the lists across the project, the record of the stage being looked at, the departments, what waits by department, and then what is set once (the departments on the project, engagements, the map and the key facts) and the recent activity. Requests, open findings and decisions waiting are not listed again on Overview: they are on each department's Documents, on Requests and on their registers. Somebody working from a grant is shown the lines their copy can answer, and never the firm's papers owed.

**Papers owed belong to a department.** The list starts from what the project has done: what it asked somebody for, the lines of a list it loaded, and the fields of its sheets that another party owes and that are due at the step it has reached. A document a check would expect that nobody has asked for is not owed by anybody and is counted nowhere; it is returned beside the list (`expected`) to ask for. A project has no page of its own for them. What is owed is one list for each department, on the Documents part of that department's functions, where a list is loaded, papers are asked for, a reply is confirmed, a line is marked in hand, and the list is copied or exported. Whatever counts papers across departments opens the department that holds them: the Overview line, a notice that a request or a line was answered, the Portfolio's requests and the cross-project figures. A request made by hand that names no line of a list and no document a check expects belongs to no department; it is made and worked on the sidebar's Requests page, which lists replies to confirm first, then mail from outside that named no request (the To sort tray). In the chat, "open Papers owed", "open the checklist" and "open requests" open the Documents of the department a person is in; from Overview they open the department whose loaded list has the most still owed, and name no page where no list is loaded.

**What a stage shows.** A function shows at the stages it has work in. They are the example project's, function for function, and a test holds the two lists together: Title, Approvals and Site in all four, RERA from Pre-construction on, Safety only while the building goes up, Procurement's four only in the two middle stages. So Legal, Finance, Engineering and Commercial are in the menu at every stage, and Procurement at two. What a function hands over at a stage is narrower than the work it has there: Title is worked on in all four stages and delivers a title opinion at Land and the conveyance at Completed. A department's Summary names a function's deliverable for the stage being looked at ("Delivers at Land: …") where it has one, and says nothing where it has none. The selector lists a department while one of its functions shows, and a department's Summary lists the same functions as its tabs. Design shows inside Engineering while it is switched on, at the stages its four workstreams have work in. At the stage the project stands at, a function that already holds the project's records also shows, whatever its stages: an answered check, a document in hand, an approval, a milestone, a site visit, a site entry, a questionnaire, a certified report, or an estimate with enough on file to say something. Work already done is never missing from the screen a person lands on. When a stage is pressed, the page on screen stays if it shows at that stage. A function that does not show there gives way to its department's Summary, and a department with nothing there to Overview. Overview and the shared places stay where they are. A link that names a function, from the chat, an alert or a card, always opens it: at the stage being looked at when the function shows there, otherwise at the project's own stage, otherwise at the first stage it has work in. The records inside a page are not yet narrowed to the stage.

**A function is one page.** A built function (Title, Approvals, Progress, Budget) is one scrolling page with a rail of its parts down the left: the estimate beside the certified report, the work that is its own (the chain of title, the approvals register, the progress board, the cost report), its checks, its documents (with the department's papers owed above them) and its connections. The rail is a column of icons that opens to show the names on hover; on a phone it lies across the top.

**Fields.** Every function but one keeps fixed, typed fields: about 3,400 of them in 288 sets across 48 functions, with 73 situations. A person fills or links them; a paper or a named public source offers values for them; a model writes none of the words that are kept.

- **A function opens on Needs you and its list of sheets, and nothing else.** At most five lines in all, the most pressing first: Decide (two values in dispute, a situation a paper suggests), Accept (values offered and waiting), Failing, Due soon and Missing. Each group is a chip with its count, and one Show all lifts the cap. Every line has one action, Accept or Open, and Open lands on the field's box. The function's other parts (its certified reports, its own board, checks, papers, connections) are on the rail, drawn one at a time. On a phone Needs you is the first card on the screen. A lead or signer may postpone a Due-soon line until a day they name.
- **An opened sheet is a form.** What a person fills is a labelled box with a clear border, its unit inside it and an example of the form expected while it is empty. What is worked out, read from another function or a standing is a flat row with a small tag, not a box. A value waiting to be accepted sits in its box with Accept and Not this attached and the paper under it.
- **Below it, each sheet is one line**: its title, how full it is, what is still to fill and where it stands. It unfolds when pressed. A sheet whose fields are not in play yet is named in a sentence and not drawn. What repeats (owners, approvals, variations, units) is a list of records, each a small sheet of its own, and a row's hard case (a minor owner's guardianship) opens as a sheet from that row alone.
- **A situation brings fields into play.** Joint development, a lender's funding, a revised sanction, litigation pending. Most are worked out from what is recorded and nobody is asked; a few are suggested by a paper and a person says yes or no. Nothing a situation hides is counted as missing, and nothing is deleted when it goes out of play.
- **A value somebody offered counts for nothing until a person accepts it.** A model's value, a parser's, another paper's: each waits on its field. No figure is worked out from it, no rule fires on it, no report prints it, until a lead or signer of the function's department accepts it. A record a paper offered (a new owner, a new purchase) is one line to accept and asks nothing more until it is. The same holds where a paper's value is told as a fact: the Approvals card, the chat and Valuation tell only what a person accepted, and say of the rest that it waits.
- **A fact has one home.** One department owns a fact and the others read it: a unit is one record on which Sales, Tax, the Architect and the site each keep their own facts, and each sees its own. Where two departments hold a figure that should agree (receivables against the demands Sales keeps, a security's value against the valuation), a rule says so when they differ.
- **Figures a law sets are rows in a rule table**, each with its instrument and the day it took effect. **None of the 187 built-in rows is verified against its instrument.** Many were written from secondary sources or from memory, and say so. Every figure worked out from one is marked not verified on the sheet, in a report and in the chat, until a named person verifies the row. Ten limits (FAR by table, ground coverage, height, parking and others) have no figure at all, and their standings say "Limit not on file".
- **What a paper offers.** 168 of the fields name a kind of document that offers their value, across 39 kinds, and one fact of one paper has one field. A paper is filed under its own name and kind, in the function that holds that kind, and never into a row that only resembles it. **By the rules alone, with no model,** values are read from the sale deed, the encumbrance certificate, the RTC, the conversion order, the plan sanction and a revised one, and the commencement certificate; land a paper states in acres and guntas is shown in those units with the square metres beside them. **Only with a model configured** are the joint development agreement, power of attorney, loan sanction letter, disbursement advice, bank guarantee, work order, variation claim, extension-of-time claim, allotment letter, agreement for sale and demand letter read into their fields: the reader is told the paper's kind and the keys of the fields that wait, and a value whose words are not found on its page is dropped. That path is tested with a stand-in model on the test machine and has not been run against a real one since it was built. Not read into any field yet: a bid's price (its earnest money is), a running bill, a payment receipt, an occupancy certificate's facts onto the sheets, and a second tranche or instalment of a sanction letter. A letter that is both a variation notice and an extension-of-time claim is read as a variation claim only.
- **A firm adds its own fields** to a built-in sheet, and folds a built-in field away, on Set up › Fields. It cannot hide or change a built-in field. Firm fields apply to sheets, not to checks.
- **What is not there.** Tax's sheets are a record for the accountant, not advice. Ledgers are views and reconciliations; the accounting package stays the source of record. The existing checks and bespoke screens (the Value tab, the Cost Report, the milestones, tenders) still hold their own figures beside the sheets: sheets read them and are not yet the place they are entered. A shared record's own rule lines and expiring dates are not raised on a function's Needs you; a value offered to a shared record is, under the function that owns that fact. A value waiting on a check shows on the function's Needs you only where one of its sheets mirrors that field of the check.

**Finance › Budget.** The part of the page that is Budget's own is the **Cost Report**. A Cost Report is started for a month and is a draft until it is issued. Its package ledger has one row for each work package, with four figures: the budget, the purchase orders issued, the amount paid, and what the package is now expected to cost when it is finished. A row also holds what a cost consultant's report states of a package, each only where it is stated: what is certified to date, as a figure of its own beside what is paid; who the package was awarded to; and variations to date. A row that is a contingency or the consultant's own fee is marked as that, and is never listed as a package not yet awarded. Under the ledger the **bottom line** is worked out and typed by nobody: the total, GST on it at the rate a rule row gives for a works contract (cited, and said to be unchecked until a person has checked it), the total with GST, and the cost for each square foot where the project's built-up area is on file. Two sheets read the same rows and are never typed: budget against PO issued against amount paid, and budget against anticipated cost with the variance. A project's first report starts from the work packages it already keeps, each with its budget and nothing ordered or paid. A report for a later month starts from the rows of the last report issued for an earlier period (the same packages with their budget, PO issued, amount paid and anticipated cost), so nothing has moved until a figure is changed; its variation log, typed reasons, basic prices, photographs and notes start empty, a package taken off the project's work packages since is left out, and the issued report is not touched. The ledger is typed row by row, or imported: a table pasted as the consultant lays it out, or a CSV or Excel file. The import knows a consultant's headings (Packages, Description, Budget, Awarded value, Awarded to, Anticipated final cost, Certified to date or Amount certified, Cost per sq ft) and reads a table with no heading row in the usual order of one, saying that it did. A dash or a blank is no amount. A section heading, a sub-total, the total, GST and the grand total are each read as what they are and are never imported as packages. Nothing is written at once: the import first shows the rows it would take, the lines it did not take and why, and whether the rows add up, to the rupee, to the table's own total; a mismatch is said with the difference, and the person confirms or cancels. A row is matched to one already there by its name. **A cost report filed as a paper** (dropped in the chat or on the page) is recognised as a cost report and filed under Budget; its tables are read from the paper's text, or by the bundled OCR where a page is a picture, and each package row is offered above the ledger with the page it is on. A lead or signer of Finance accepts a row, or all the rows of a page, and may correct a cell first; a row whose figures could not be put in their columns shows the page's own line to type from. Where no row could be read because the tables are pictures and no model reader is set up, the page says so in one line and opens the paste box with the PDF beside it. A row's name, code and budget are kept the same on the project's work package, so the graph draws it. Under the ledger are a variation log (what moved the cost, by how much, and whether it was drawn from contingency), basic price adjustments (tender rate against current rate, with the difference where a quantity is given), the photographs chosen for the period, and a note. Above the ledger, **Since last report** reads the report against the last one issued for an earlier period: the total forecast then and now, the three packages that moved most with what accounts for each (a variation logged against the package, or a package awarded since), whatever no variation or award accounts for as its own unexplained amount, the running bills dated in between as claimed against certified, and the decisions needed (packages forecast over budget, packages with no PO). Whoever fills the ledger can type one line of reason against a package's change. Issuing freezes the report under the name of whoever issued it, and it prints as a document of its own, a cover and the sheets. An issued report is never edited: to correct a figure a lead or signer opens a revision, a draft beside it for the same month with a copy of its figures, and the issued report goes on being the one that is read until the revision is issued in its turn. The first is then kept as it was sent. A lead, contributor or signer in Finance fills the ledger; issuing and revising are for the lead or signer.

Below the Cost Report is the register of contracts and running bills: a contract with a contractor against work packages, a bill under it with its lines, an amount passed line by line, a certificate issued on the bill under a signer's name with its deductions, and the payments recorded against it. Issuing or withdrawing a certificate, voiding a payment and removing a bill or a contract are for the lead or signer. What is certified counts only under a certificate that stands.

What Budget does not do yet: every figure on a Cost Report is entered, or accepted from a row a cost report offered, and none is worked out from the bills; a cost report's variation log and basic prices are not read from the paper; nothing reads a bill's own file into the register; no alert is raised from either; and the chat and the status report do not read a Cost Report's figures (the owner pack reads an issued one) (the chat reaches the work packages, contracts, bills and certificates through the graph). An outside collaborator sees none of it unless the budget is ticked on their grant.

**How well a table that is a picture is read.** Measured on an invented cost report drawn at 300 dpi (eleven packages in six ruled columns, with shaded heading rows): the bundled OCR read every figure of every row once the table's rules were lifted from the picture, and the rows added up to the page's total to the rupee; read with the rules on, half the rows were lost or had a digit changed. One name came back a letter out. A scanned, photographed or compressed table will read worse than a clean drawn one, which is why every row is offered and none is written, why a page's rows are held to its own total, and why a person may correct a cell. Where a model reader is set up it is shown only the pages nobody here got a row from, answers in the ledger's column keys with each figure as the page writes it, and a row any figure of which is not among the words this server read on that page is offered as unverified and is never taken with the rest of its page.

**Engineering › Technical: report observations.** A technical due diligence, condition or structural audit report is recognised as a report and filed under Technical. Its observations are offered there as rows of **Report observations**: what was seen and what is recommended in the report's own words, the risk and the part of the building as picks from a list, each with its page. They wait for a lead or signer of Engineering like any offered value. The rules read observations written with labels (Observation, Risk category, Mitigation) and as the numbered rows of a table; a report laid out another way is filed and offers none.

**A dropped file nobody said what to do with.** Where the chat cannot tell whether a file is a questionnaire or a paper it asks, with the two answers to press. Such a file is not being read, and no page says that it is: the notice at the top of the project shows the question while an answer is waited for (ten minutes), and then goes. The file is kept as a paper to file, listed under Documents with the same answers to press, so the question can be answered there on any later day; read again with no answer given it is read as a paper, and the question is not put twice. A paper a reading left behind can be read or set aside from the notice.

**The site map.** The map on Site is drawn from the state's own published land map, read by survey number, and a site that stands on many survey numbers is read for each. Under the place picker is one line for every number the file knows: the project's own, each one a document was read as stating (with the document, and whether a person has accepted that reading or it still waits), and any a person types, several at once. What a person typed comes first; the rest are in the order of the numbers, the parts of one survey number together. A zero before a part is the same number, so 77/03 and 77/3 are one line naming both papers; it is asked of the map as 77/3, and again as the paper writes it where a state's map does not hold it so. A number that looks like another the papers state with its stroke lost in reading, 472 beside 47/2 where no whole number in the papers is that long, is said to be a likely misreading while nobody has accepted that reading, and waits unticked; a person can still have it read, and a number a person has accepted is a number like any other. An accepted number is ticked; one that waits can be ticked too, and then says it was read by machine and not yet accepted. Each ticked number is read on a request of its own, in turn, and its line says where it stands: reading, read with its area, not in the published map with the numbers that start the same way, or failed with the reason. One failure does not stop the rest, though a run ends when the map fails to answer three numbers running, and waits when the server asks it to; a number already read is read again only when asked. A range, or two numbers run together, is shown as it was written and not read, with how to write it; a dash from a lower number to a higher is read as a range, and the line says how to write a part of a number. Every read is kept, one for each parcel, and can be removed singly or all together. The first is kept whole and each one after it keeps the outlines of the tanks and streams round it once for the site, so a parcel more weighs under a fifth of what the first does. A read that would make the project's record too heavy to open is not kept, a parcel read again among them where the fresh read is heavier: its line says how many parcels are kept and why, and the run stops there. The map frames all the parcels, draws each outline with its survey number on it, and draws the state's water, drains and planning zones round them once; a finding about one parcel names its number. Under the map each parcel's area from its outline is listed beside the extent the register records for it, with the total. What the documents were accepted as stating is set beside the map like for like: the extents of documents about different survey numbers are added up, they are set against the parcels for those numbers and no others, and only once every one of those numbers is read; until then it says how many are. An extent whose document names no survey number is taken as the whole site's, and set against every parcel read once every number the file states is among them; where several such documents do not agree, none is taken for the site. A deed read off the page as naming one number may be for more: where its figure is more than that parcel holds, nothing is set against it until every number the file states is read, and where it is then the figure of every parcel read it is set against them all. Nothing is set against a document that states a range, or a number two parcels read both carry, and it says why. It is two figures and the difference between them, raised when they are a twentieth apart or more; the lender's check of the papers on Value counts the same land the same way, so deeds for different survey numbers are added up there too, not set against each other. The valuation is offered that total as one candidate for the land area, last in rank, and one guidance value, with the parcel it was read for; where the parcels carry different values the guideline value of the plot counts each at its own, and none is given for a site when one of its parcels carries no published value, whether the others agree or not: which parcel that is, is said. An outline from the state's map is a government record read by machine: not a survey, and not evidence until a person attaches the extract. Karnataka's map holds whole survey numbers, so 41/1 and 41/2 are both answered by the parcel for 41, which is counted once and says it is the whole number wherever it is shown; more land on the map than a document, the land area on the project or a person's own outline gives for a part of a number is said, not raised, up to that parcel's own area. Two parcels with one number in two villages are told apart by their villages.

**The example project.** *Lakeview Tower* is a whole project with made-up data, open to anybody at `/example` without signing in and reached from the landing page and the portfolio. It shows all twenty-eight functions of the five departments at each of the four stages, each as its own page: the papers it expects, its values with the page each was read from, tables, photographs, maps with water, drains and their buffers, town-planning zones, roads, the power line and the airport zone, what a proper project expects of it, its flags, the copilot's insights, and the law or standard it rests on. A value the copilot read is accepted or rejected on the field or in the proof pane beside it; a paper opens in a pop-up; a paper or a map has one home and is referred to from the other functions that use it. Nothing in it is saved, and its copilot's replies are scripted. A function or department that is not built yet links from a real project to its page there.

**Colour.** Teal marks what is picked or can be followed; blue marks what the copilot wrote or suggested and no person has accepted yet; green, amber and red are kept for status.

- **Observations and mitigations** (step 4) is the table the report is read for: area by area, what was seen, its risk category, the mitigation, the code it is judged against, and the photographs that show it. An observation is a finding — the same record a check raises or the chat proposes — so nothing is entered twice. Below the table sit the photographs nothing cites yet, from the document register and from the phone's site log, each with what a model saw in it and any finding it suggested; one press starts an observation from a photograph or attaches it to one. A site-log photograph is filed onto the register the first time it is used.
- **Photographs** (step 3) is a contact sheet of every photograph on the project, from the document register and the phone's site log. Each has a caption and an area to fill in, a description, and one switch for whether it prints in the report. A photograph an observation cites is in the report with that observation, and says so. The description works the way a value read off a document does: a model's reading (what is visible, never a cause or a severity) arrives on the photograph as a suggestion, a person accepts it or edits it, and only then is it the photograph's description and printed in the report. A person can also write it without asking a model.
- **What prints is chosen where the work is.** An observation, a photograph and a question each carry an *In report* switch; switching one off keeps it on the file and leaves it out of the report. Whole sections are moved or removed in the report itself.
- **Report** (step 5) creates the **technical due diligence report** and opens it. It starts with the sections an engineer's report has — the property, scope and basis, building information, observations and mitigations, remedial cost, the inspection record, documents reviewed and outstanding, limitations, opinion — and three of them are tables that read the steps before: the answered questionnaire (a suggestion nobody confirmed is printed as not answered, and answers resting on the seller's word alone are counted), the observations with their risk, mitigation and reference, and the document sheet with what was not received. The photographs each observation cites print under the table, captioned with the row, the date and the coordinates. An **at a glance** section counts the observations by risk category, with a bar for each. A **site photographs** section prints the photographs chosen for the report that no observation already shows. The tables print the same on screen, on paper and in the Word file, and an issued report keeps them as they stood. The three tables also export on their own.

The pieces under the other steps:

- **Papers owed** (step 1): one list of every paper the desk is waiting for, by who owes it. Two records feed it and stay two records: the documents the checks expect, and a list of papers somebody outside sent. A line stands in one of four places: not asked, asked for (with the day it was asked and the day it is due), answered and waiting for a person to confirm, or in hand. Whoever has a reply waiting comes first, then whoever is overdue, and the lines nobody is named for come last; a long group is read under its own headings (the discipline a check sits in, or the stage and package of a list). Tick lines, name who is asked for the ones nobody is named for and by when, and one press asks: one tracked request for a document a check expects, and one request to each party for everything of a list it owes. A reply that waits shows on its line with Confirm and Set aside. A line turns to in hand on its own when the paper is filed.
- **A list that was sent** is loaded as it was written from pasted text, a CSV or TSV, or an Excel workbook. A row that names the columns (item, stage, package, owed by) is used where there is one, and a heading sets the stage or the package for the lines under it. Loading the same list again adds nothing. Lines are added, renamed, moved and removed one at a time. A line is in hand when a filed document carries its name or a person marks it, with the document where they name one. A document that only looks like it is offered under the line and a person confirms or refuses it. A request made for a list's lines closes when all of them are in hand. A list belongs to the department whose desk it was loaded on, and is changed by the firm's people who may edit there. The list copies as text to paste into a message, shows only the lines standing one way when asked, and exports as one spreadsheet.
- **The same card**, narrowed to the department, is on the Documents part of each built function laid out as one page: Title and Approvals show Legal's, Progress shows Engineering's beside this step, and Budget shows Finance's. A desk whose checks are not started says so and starts them there. An outside collaborator is handed no list and none of the firm's requests, so on a department they were given they see the documents its checks expect and nothing else.
- **The charts** under Observations: findings and checks by discipline, and what the remedies cost by how soon they are needed.
- **Supporting documents**: the client's deeds, approvals and valuation still arrive. They are read and citable, and show on the department page as supporting documents; one can be given to a workstream here. Switching Legal or Finance on later puts each where it belongs.

- **The questionnaire** (step 2): the client's own list of questions about the building, imported as written from Excel, Word, PDF, a spreadsheet saved as CSV, plain text, or pasted text. One dropped in the chat is taken in the same way. Answers already in the sheet come in as the seller's. Each answer records where it came from — the seller said so, a document states it, it was seen on site, or it is the engineer's view — and the documents or photographs that stand behind it, with the page. The chat can answer from the documents on file; its answers arrive as suggestions and wait for a person to confirm them, one by one or all at once. The answered sheet copies as text and exports as Excel or PDF, in the order the questions came in, each answer with where it came from and the paper and page behind it.

The chat does all of this too: it reads the documents the checks expect and the loaded list, the questionnaire and the technical summary, and proposes asking for documents, moving a document, or changing the departments (the last for an admin to approve).

**Workstreams (the work inside).** Each workstream holds, stage by stage:

- a **quick assessment** — a living estimate, built from the project's own findings and data with AI help, following the official method for that kind of assessment, re-read whenever anything changes;
- **certified reports** from named professionals — the figure of record;
- its **checks** and **records** (approvals, milestones, site entries, comparables…);
- the **documents** it owns, from the one shared vault.

**Engagements (who it's for).** A piece of work a client commissions — acquisition screening, title due diligence, technical due diligence, a lender's independent engineer, an indicative valuation. An engagement draws on workstreams and produces one deliverable. Checks live once, in their workstream, so the same title check serves every engagement that needs it.

**Procurement › Tenders** keeps one tender for each work package of the cost register. It moves through nine stages in order: tender documents ready, issued to bidders, queries and addenda, bids received, comparison, negotiation, recommendation, letter of acceptance, contract signed. Each has a date and an owner; queries and negotiation may be marked not needed. A stage already done cannot be re-dated to after a later stage that is done. A stage marked not needed does not constrain other stages' due dates. Each bidder is kept with the amount quoted, the amount revised after negotiation, qualifications, exclusions and, optionally, priced items. Bids are set against the pre-tender estimate and against the median of the bids, the bid's own included. A bid more than 15% from the median, or a priced item more than 25% from it, is flagged once there are three bids, in the words "differs from the others by X%" and never as a judgement. A flagged bid is written with enough decimals to read past its limit (15.01%, never 15%). The recommendation is written by a person, under their name. Recording a letter of acceptance offers the awarded value to the package's row on the draft Cost Report as PO issued; it is written there, in place of what stood, only when a lead or signer in Finance accepts it, and with no draft Cost Report holding the package the offer waits. A stage past its date raises an alert for Procurement's lead and the stage's owner. A tender needs a work package from Finance › Budget first. Tenders are not drawn in the graph, so the chat opens the page by name and cannot answer about bids. An outside collaborator sees tenders only when their grant names Procurement.

## Quick assessments and certified reports

A quick assessment fills its inputs in this order, and a higher source always overrides a lower one:

1. Verified data on the file
2. The project's own documents
3. Government records, laws and policies (registered sales, guidance values)
4. Regulatory and professional standards (IBBI, RICS, IS codes, NBC)
5. Licensed transaction data
6. Portal listings (asking prices)
7. Reputable published sources
8. Standard assumptions

Every input shows its source. Assumptions are labelled; a figure resting more than half on them shows only as a rough range. Two sources at the same level that disagree are shown side by side for a person to pick.

A certified report — a title opinion, a valuation, a progress certificate — is uploaded by its signer and read for its figures, signer, registration, date and scope. It becomes the figure of record. The quick assessment keeps running beside it, and the report is flagged **for revisiting** when later evidence moves the estimate:

- a figure more than 10% from the certified one;
- progress more than 5 points from the certified progress;
- any new blocker, or a new condition where the report was clear.

The flag goes to the department's lead and the professional who signed.

## People and access

Roles per department: **Lead** (runs it, accepts proposals, owns deadlines), **Contributor** (adds documents, records, site entries), **Signer** (certifies its reports; their registration is recorded), **Viewer**. A person can hold different roles in different departments. Firm roles set the default: owners and managers lead every department, staff contribute, viewers read. The project's team list says otherwise where it needs to, and outside collaborators reach only the departments they are given.

**Who accepts what was read.** Only a lead or a signer of the department a value belongs to may accept it, correct it, set it aside or reopen it, pick one of two readings that disagree, or move the document out of the function that holds it. A contributor files documents and records what they find, and decides nothing that was read. The server holds every way in to this rule: the document's own row, a card on a check, the Value tab, an approval typed or pressed in the chat, a step of a plan. A page route answers 403 and names the department; the chat takes what the person may decide and says what it left, and for which department. Staff are contributors by default, so they are made a lead or a signer on the project's team list before they accept. What the rule leaves open is listed in [auth.md](auth.md#who-decides-what-a-document-states): a value typed onto a check by hand, a finding or a suggested answer accepted from a card, marking a row rejected, a comparable added by hand, and undo.

## One chat, and what it may do

One chat on every project, aware of the page you are on. The chat and the links between departments **propose**; a person **approves** where the proposal lands. Anything that leaves the system — an email, an invoice, a filing, money — needs a named approver. Internal alerts fire on their own.

**It goes where you say.** A department, a function, a shared place or a stage named in a sentence opens: "open Approvals", "show Legal at Land", "the checks for Approvals", "back to the live stage". No model is needed, and the reply is one line saying where it went, the stage when that changed, and the figure that matters there. A name two pages share ("Handover" is Legal's and Commercial's) is asked back as a choice, and a department that is switched off says so. A short name nothing has is said to be unknown, and nothing moves; a longer sentence that holds a shared place's word among others still opens that place. A name in quotes is a record's first, and a page's only when no record has it. Asking to see a stage never moves the project to it; only a sentence that says the project has moved, or is to be moved, does. A question carried in a link waits in the message box for the person to send.

**Links land where the record lives.** A record named in an answer, a citation or a chip under a reply opens the page of the function that holds it, at its part of that page. A document, a check, an approval, a milestone and a site entry are marked there; an estimate and a certified report open their part of the page. A record no function's page holds opens in its register. Where the department is switched off, a document or a check opens in its register, a certified report opens as the paper it is, and anything else opens in the graph. A stage pressed in an answer changes the stage in view as pressing the stage track does, and a check or a document that is open stays open.

**It answers about the page.** On a function's page, "what's missing", "summarise" and "which findings are critical" are answered for that function and say so ("On Title: …"); asking about the whole project widens the answer. The questions offered under the box are the page's own on the built functions, each department's Summary and the shared registers; anywhere else they follow from where the file stands. Moving between pages keeps the thread, and a question shows where it was asked when that is not the page in view. An outside collaborator is told a function's or a department's page opened without its figure, and an answer about a page opens with "Of what you have been given" and leaves out the firm's estimate.

**Earlier chats.** One control in the chat's header lists the chats on the project, each by its first question, its day and the page it started on, with a search once there are more than eight. A chat that opens with papers dropped in is listed by what was read. An earlier chat opens to read, and can be continued or renamed. The last line of the list lets the firm's own people delete every chat on the project, for everyone, after being asked once.

**A document dropped in.** The reply says where each paper was filed: the function and the stage, or Documents when no function holds it. For a paper no function holds, the likely functions are offered and none is picked; pressing one files the paper there at once, and it can be undone. An outside collaborator is offered none, because giving a paper to a function is for the firm's own people. The canvas opens the function's documents with the new row marked. The reply then says how many of the paper's values go to which function's checks, and calls out a value that differs from one on a check, on the project record or on another accepted paper, with both values and where each came from. Two areas or lengths within a unit and half a percent of each other are one measure rounded twice, and any other number has to be the same number. A paper for one parcel of a site does not differ from the site, and a value a person has already decided for a paper is not raised again for that paper. When the paper brings a value, a flag or a difference, the firm's own people are also told what it reaches downstream, including a certified report that rests on it. Nothing a paper states is on the file until a person accepts it, and a differing value never overwrites the one recorded. One chip opens the paper in the graph.

**Approving in a sentence.** Typed words accept in three ways, and only as the whole sentence: "approve all" (or "accept everything", "approve both", "approve them") takes what the last reply of that chat raised and the values on the papers it filed; "approve all open" (or "approve everything waiting") takes every card open on the project, for the firm's own people; and "approve", "accept", "skip", "reject" or "set aside" with a card's exact title in quotes takes or sets aside that one card, when the last reply listed it. A bare "yes", "ok" or "skip", a card's name without quotes, a question, and a sentence with a no or a later in it take nothing; where the last reply left something waiting, the reply says what waits and where, and offers it as choices to press. A choice carries the cards and papers it means, so pressing one acts on exactly those, only while they still wait, and it is a button only under the last reply of the chat on screen.

**It answers with its sources.** A citation sits in the sentence it supports, as a chip that carries the paper's name and the page. Pressing it opens the paper at that page. The paper opens over the work surface and leaves the conversation beside it; Escape closes it and returns to where the person was. A citation to a record that is not on the file stays on screen and opens nothing, so a claim never looks sourced when it is not. The record is asked before any page: a value the project already holds (the city, the address, the assessment year) is said from the record, and under an answer given by rule the chat says where the facts it is about stand, each as approved by a named person or still waiting. "What do we know about…", "what was decided", "what is still undecided" and "what changed" are answered from the project's memory, with no model. The detail is in [architecture.md](architecture.md#the-projects-memory).

**What goes into it.** Files reach the chat by the paperclip, the camera, the microphone, a paste, or a drop anywhere on the chat's column. A paper is filed as above. A list of questions somebody sent is taken in as a questionnaire, with answers suggested from the file; its chip opens the Questions step at that sheet. The notes of a meeting are kept as a meeting, and what they decided, what is to be done and what was left open wait on cards. A voice note is put into words where a transcription model is set up, and becomes a proposed entry on the site log, each line beside the words it came from. A photograph of the site is filed to Progress. A file keeps its name whatever script the name is written in.

**A question can be spoken.** Where the browser has speech recognition (Chrome, Edge, Safari; not Firefox) the message box has a microphone for asking and a language choice: English (India), Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu and the browser's own languages. The words appear in the box and are sent only when the person sends them. The browser turns the speech into text and may send the sound to its maker; the first use says so. The choice of language is kept per person on the device. Listening takes one stretch of speech and stops by itself, so a long question takes a second press.

**An answer can be read aloud** by the browser, in a voice for the answer's language when the device has one. Citations are not read; their count is ("Three sources."), and one is said in the singular in each offered language. An empty table cell is skipped, and so is a sentence that only pointed at its citations. Nothing plays until Read aloud is pressed, and Stop reading stops it. A device with voices but none for the answer's language says so and reads nothing.

**It answers with a card, in the language asked.** With a model configured, the model does not write the answer. It picks the fields, records, memory lines and passages that answer, and the reply is a card printed by code: each value as the record holds it, where it stands (accepted, entered, waiting on a person, worked out) and its source. A figure worked out from a rule row names the row and says it is not verified. Where the record does not answer, the card lists the fields that would hold the answer and the papers that would state it. The one line said round the card is a template, in English, Hindi or Kannada; Tamil, Telugu and other languages fall back to English, and the Hindi and Kannada lines have not been checked by a native reader. With no model the chat answers by rule, in English. None of this has been tried with a real model or real speech.

**A job with many steps is shown first.** An instruction that comes to several steps, or to one step over more than ten records, is laid out as a plan with what each step will touch. Nothing starts until a person runs it; it can be stopped and taken up again; and what its steps raise still waits for a person where it waited before.

**Every change can be undone.** A reply that changed the record says what it changed, and its author can undo that message on its own, later, without taking back anything done since.

**Reports and letters from a sentence.** "Write this week's status for the owner" drafts a status report for the period named: what changed, what waits and on whom, and what comes next, every line with the record behind it. It is made by code from the record, so no model adds a line, and no model rewords one. A letter, a reply to a paper and a request for information are the one place prose remains: each is one of six built-in templates, whose fixed sentences are filled from the record's own lines; a model only chooses the template and which numbered sources fill it, and a lead or signer edits and approves by name before it is exported as a Word file. A firm cannot yet edit the templates. Minutes of a meeting and a voice note are kept as the words quoted from them. The app sends nothing itself.

**Months and quarters.** The status report and the owner pack cover a month or a quarter. Quarters run from the first of January, April, July and October and are numbered in the Indian financial year by default (July to September 2026 is Q2 FY 2026-27); a project's report day may say the calendar year (Q3 2026). The chat writes a status report for "last quarter", "this quarter", "Q2 FY 2026-27" or "Q3 2026". A numbered quarter wins over the word "quarterly", and a year written 2026-27 is read as a financial year. Today, for tender stages, overdue actions and requests, the current quarter and the owner pack's default month, is the day in India. A period's ends are UTC month boundaries, so something recorded between midnight and 05:30 India time on the 1st falls in the month before.

**The Reports page** opens with the owner pack, because checking and issuing it is what is done there each month: a pack that waits has Check and issue beside it, and opens as the paper it is, where it is issued under a name. With nothing waiting, the pack for a month or a quarter is opened from the same card. Issued packs are listed under it, and the report day is its last line. Status, due diligence and function reports, a link to the Cost Report on Finance › Budget, and letters follow.

**A function's report is put together from its fields.** Each department declares reports of its own, nineteen in all (a title report, an approvals status report, a RERA compliance report, a valuation summary, a development appraisal, a cost-to-complete report, a lender's pack, a monthly progress report, a contracts and claims report, a sales and collections report and others), each an ordered list of fields, tables of records, charts and one capped note for a person's own words. A value prints with a numbered footnote saying where it came from; a value that waits prints "waiting" and never its figure; a figure from a rule row prints the row's citation and "not verified". A person adds field sections, moves and removes them, and writes notes capped at 2,000 characters that carry their name and the day. A section detached from its fields is frozen as it read that day. No model writes or rewords any line of a report. An outside collaborator's copy of a report carries no buyer, no list of parties kept as it read one day, and no section naming a field out of their reach.

**An owner pack can be kept.** A kept pack is the pack as it read when it was prepared; issued by a lead or a signer of any department on the project it is frozen with their name and the time, and opens exactly as issued whatever the record has done since. Only an issued pack can be reopened, by a lead or a signer. Reopening leaves the issued pack exactly as it was sent and keeps a copy beside it, waiting (the one already waiting for that period, if there is one), to be read again and issued in its turn. An issued quarterly pack keeps the quarter number it was issued under if the project later changes how it numbers quarters; a pack kept before this was stored prints the label the project numbers quarters by now. A pack kept again for a period that has an issued one is a new pack beside it. A kept pack cannot be deleted, and a project keeps at most 60.

**A report day.** A project may set a report day: a day of the month, in India time, on which the status report, the owner pack or both are prepared for the month just ended, or for the quarter just ended in January, April, July and October. In a month shorter than the day, the day is the month's last. What is prepared waits at the top of the Reports page marked "Prepared, waiting to be issued", with Check and issue beside it, and the firm's own people named in the setting are told by email, where a mail key is set, and on their phones. The timer issues nothing and sends nothing to an owner. If that day's run is missed, the next run prepares the same period, provided the report day was already set on the missed day; a report day set after its date this month prepares nothing until the next one. People are recorded as told only once somebody was; if the mail was refused, the next run tells them. A status report the timer prepared is an ordinary draft that reads the record until a person issues it; only the owner pack is a snapshot from the moment it was prepared. Somebody working from a grant with the reports area does not see a report the timer prepared until a person issues it, and an issued status report reaches them without the checklist's lines.

**Reports by name.** The chat opens the Reports page for "open the owner pack", and a kept owner pack or a report by its name ("open the owner pack for September 2026", "show me the status report for September 2026"), saying whether it is prepared and waiting or issued and by whom.

**A loaded list of papers owed** is read by the chat's requirement tool, appears in the status report under what is waiting (one line for each party, for papers not in hand and not asked for; papers asked for are already there as requests), and exports as a CSV beside the one for what the checks expect, on the Report step of the department desk. The status report still calls it the checklist: an issued report keeps its words, and the rule that leaves those lines out of a collaborator's copy knows them by those words. Every CSV here opens in Excel with Indian scripts intact, and no cell can run as a formula.

**An empty form is filled from the record.** On a department's Questions step a Word (.docx) or Excel (.xlsx) form is uploaded and its blanks are found by code: brackets, runs of underscores or dots, a label with nothing after it, empty table cells beside or under a label, content controls and merge fields in Word; empty cells beside or under a label and named cells in a workbook. Each blank is offered a value that stands on the file, with the paper and page or the project record entry it came from and whether a named person approved it. A blank nothing answers is left empty and listed as having no source. Only what a person accepts or types is written. The same file goes back with its formatting kept, beside a PDF list of sources. Where a model is set up it may say which of the record's values a blank asks for, by key, only for a blank whose label the rules could not read; it is shown the labels and nothing of the record, and is never asked for a value. Not built: PDF forms, blanks in a Word header, footer or footnote, tick boxes and dropdowns. A workbook is written back through a library that keeps cell formats, merges and names but may drop charts and drawings. A merged answer box is one blank, written at its first cell. Blanks on hidden sheets are not offered or written. A plain number accepted for a cell with a number format, or none, is written as a number so formulas over it work; anything else is written as text and never as a formula. Labels are found in any script, including Hindi and Kannada. A workbook is refused when its sheets unpack to more than 4 MB, and also when what it carries beside its sheets unpacks to more than 16 MB. Where several papers state different values for one blank the newest is offered and the screen has no picker for the others.

## Links between departments

Live among the built functions: title and approvals feed the valuation's drivers and lender checks; approvals gate construction (work logged without a commencement certificate is flagged); progress feeds the as-is value and the cost to complete; a tender's letter of acceptance offers its awarded value to Finance's draft Cost Report.

Among the field sheets, a department reads what another owns and types none of it twice. Law reads the land Title finds clear of restriction, Tax's payments to the landowner, Sales' agreements and the designated account's figures. Valuation reads Title's standing, the head lease and the loan outstanding. Feasibility and Budget read the agreement's share, the registration day, fees demanded and Procurement's variations. Finance holds its receivables, GST and ledgers to Sales' and Collections' records. Sales holds every launch, advertisement and allotment to the registration day, and a unit allotted to the land owners is flagged if it is released for sale. Construction reads the completion declared to RERA, stop-work orders and court restraints. Design reads the sanction, the clearances and the clear extent. Progress is read from the milestones. Of the 206 links the departments' authors asked for, 104 are made and 36 needed nothing; 66 are still open, mostly where the owner keeps no such field, a record is at its twenty fields, or a formula cannot join the two records. Those figures are typed on the reader's side until then.

## Law and policy

A firm keeps a list of official pages under **Law and policy**, a page of the firm's own people. Each is read on its own interval (1 to 90 days) by the daily timer, or when an admin presses Look now; when its words change, the lines that came and went are shown. Where a model is set up it proposes the change as fields and nothing else: the instrument, its reference and date, the kind of change, the figure before and after, what it affects (a field of the departments it bears on, an approval, or a row of the rule table) and action items by project and department, each a department, a next step off a list and what it is about. A reference, a day or a figure the changed lines do not write is dropped. There is no summary. Nothing reaches a project until a firm admin or a department's lead confirms it by name. A lead decides only for the projects they lead: the change is marked decided for those and waits for the rest until their leads decide or a firm admin closes it. Confirming and dismissing take somebody who may change a record. Each action then carries the source's link, the day the change was seen and who confirmed it. It watches the listed pages only and is not legal advice. A footer's date, a visitor counter and a clock are not a change, in English or Hindi. A page longer than 400,000 characters is cut at a whole line and says so; what is past the cut is not watched. The list every firm starts with is three central pages (two of the environment ministry's and the insolvency board's circulars); it holds no Karnataka source and no India Code source, because none answered from where it was built, so a firm adds its own. A watched page is usually a listing: the reader does not follow a link into the new circular itself, so a proposal rests on the listing's line alone. Nobody is told when a confirmed action lands on a project; it shows on the project's actions and its trail. A confirmed change of a rate offers a new dated row for the rule table, unverified; once a firm admin verifies it by name it is that firm's row, and every figure on the firm's projects worked out from that rule reads it from the day it takes effect.

## The daily review

Once a day, each active project that has changed since its last review is cross-checked. Four kinds of finding are **rules, and run with no model**: two papers that disagree on a field (only where the field names its papers), a date that cannot be right (a milestone that finishes before it starts, a validity that ends before it begins, work logged before its certificate, and every date rule a department's field set declares where a paper states one of the two dates), an approval that logged work depends on and is missing or lapsed, and an action nobody owns. For the other two (cost that moved with no reason, schedule and cost telling different stories) a model, where one is set up, only nominates which record entries to look at; code works out the difference and words the line from a template. Each suggestion cites the record entries it rests on (paper and page where a paper states them); one that cites anything not on the record is dropped, and so is one that cites no entry of the kind it is about. On a large project the brief a model reads keeps the newest values, the overdue or unowned actions first, and a share each of findings, risks and alerts. A lead or signer of its department accepts it on Registers › Risks and actions, and it becomes a finding or a risk, or dismisses it with a reason. A dismissed suggestion silences only a later proposal about the very same entries. Nobody is notified of a suggestion and none reaches a report or the chat. With no model set up the four rules still run and the two nominations do not, and the page says so. It has never been run against a real model, so how useful the nominations are is unmeasured. Spend is capped at 40,000 tokens a project a day and 600,000 a day in all.

## Alerts

In the web app, the site app and by email: approvals expiring or lapsed, certified reports to revisit, work logged before it is allowed, milestones behind, likely to finish late or late, an extension of time that looks likely, serious issues from site, an action past its date, and a tender's stage past its date (key `tender:<tenderId>:<stage>:due:<date>`, raised once for that date). An approval that is simply not on file yet is an alert in the app and is not mailed.

**Before a date is missed.** The schedule is brought in on the progress board from where it is kept: a pasted table, a CSV or an Excel file with an activity, a planned finish and, where the sheet has them, a planned start, a weight and a percent complete. Rows that could not be read are listed back with the reason. Bringing it in again moves the dates of the milestones it names, keeps the progress recorded for them, and keeps the first planned finish so a re-plan shows. From 14 days before its planned finish a milestone is behind when its progress is under the straight line between its planned start and finish, or under 80% where no start is known. With progress recorded on two days or more, a finish is projected at the current rate and flagged when it lands after the planned one; the project is likely to need an extension of time when its projected completion is after its planned completion, or that date has passed with work unfinished. One alert stands for a milestone's warning until its date, and its words follow the days left. The projection is a straight line through what was recorded, not a programme: it knows nothing of sequence, float or resources.

Email and push go to the lead and the signer of the department the alert belongs to, and an overdue action also to the person it is on. Nobody is sent what they could not read in the app. Alerts are worked out when a project is saved, and once a day for every project by a timer (01:30 UTC, 07:00 in India), so a date that comes up on a day nobody touches the project raises its alert that morning. The timer runs on the production deployment, and only where its secret is set. A new document or a new value raises no alert of its own; it raises a notice, below.

**Telling the people concerned.** When a paper is filed, a value read off it is accepted or changed from what stood, a paper owed is marked in hand or a request is answered, the people it concerns are told what it is and the one thing to do: the lead and signer of the department that holds the paper, whoever is named on an action, request, checklist line or check it answers or touches, and the leads and signers of a department whose check holds another value or that the paper's function feeds. Whoever did it is not told. The words are written by rule, with the page. A value nobody accepted, whether a model or the rules read it, is counted as waiting to be checked and never said. It lists in My work at once, under Notices; mail and push go as one message a person a project, ten minutes after the first notice, and not for a project the person has quieted. A timer sends what is due every ten minutes, so mail arrives ten to twenty minutes after the first change; without a mail key the notices stay in the app. What tells nobody: accepting a card on a check, a value typed on a check, valuation inputs and comparables; a photograph filed; and anything the server did by itself other than taking in a paper or a reply. A paper tells whichever way it came: dropped by a person, brought by a connected folder, or sent back in reply to a request by email or WhatsApp. A reply, with or without a paper, tells whoever raised the request. A send that fails is tried again by the ten-minute timer, three times in all; after that the notice stays in the app. The site app receives the push and has no screen for these notices.

## What is tracked over time

The record keeps the past of what it holds, from this release on. Nothing is migrated: a field's history begins at its first change after the release, and a day before that reads as now and says so.

- **A field keeps what it held.** Every value a field has held is kept with who made it stand and from when until when, the last twenty-four of them. Pressing a field's label says it in one line, "Was ₹4.1 Cr on 12 Aug 2026 (R. Iyer); now ₹4.32 Cr", with a small line where it has held three figures. A value somebody offered, and one in dispute, was never the field's and is not in its past.
- **Sets kept by period.** A set that is kept by the week, the month, the quarter or the year says so. A new period opens from the last with what carries over already filled and marked "Carried from September", so nothing is asked twice. A period is compared with the one before it figure by figure ("Was ₹4.32 Cr in August; now ₹3.85 Cr"); where dated rows explain a figure their sum is set against it, and what is left is "Unexplained" until a person picks why from a short list. A period is issued under a person's name and is then never edited; a correction is a revision kept beside it. The latest period that has ended and is not issued is one line under Needs you ("September: 2 of 3 fields, was due 7 Oct 2026"), and the report day's notice lists them. A period nobody kept is said to be not kept, and nothing fills it in but a person. Kept this way: cash by month, the forecast by week, GST and TDS months and statements, RERA certificates, filings, yearly statements and quarterly progress, the architect's and engineer's certificates, property tax years, and the lender's engineer reports and cost statements for each drawdown.
- **Sheets kept by period.** A sheet of live figures is the period in hand. Issuing it writes down every figure, the worked-out ones among them, as it read. Kept by the month: Construction's month at a glance and safety statistics, the cash position, receivables, the loan position, contracts and claims, collections, dues, marketing spend and the RFI log. Kept by the week: stock, bookings and enquiries. One line on the sheet opens the past ("September issued by A. Rao · 2 earlier"), and an issued period opens read only under a band that says who issued it.
- **As at a day.** A function's sheets can be read as they stood on a past day, under a band with the date and with every control off. A function's report can be made for a month that has ended: its figures are then as at that month's last day, the report says "Figures as at 30 Sep 2026", and what is recorded in October does not move it. The owner pack for a month that has ended reads progress and the schedule the same way. What the record does not reach prints as now, and says so.
- **Progress.** Planned progress is worked out from the milestones' planned dates and typed by nobody. The progress board draws planned against actual month by month, and each milestone's readings as a small line. Earned value (planned value, earned value, what is certified, and the two indices) shows on the programme sheet only where every milestone is tied to a work package with a budget, and not otherwise.
- **Trends.** A figure that is kept by period is drawn over its periods where its field says so, inside the same popover: a line, bars, or planned against actual. "Sold since the last week issued" and "Progress added since the last month issued" are worked out, and a standing warns when progress has hardly moved. Across the firm's projects, the schedule is read month by month, planned against made, and units sold week by week where a project has issued its week's stock; this view is the firm's own, and nobody working from a grant is given it.
- **Photographs of the same place over time.** The Site record keeps photo stations: a place shot again and again from the same spot, with its building, floor, element and milestone, and how often it is to be shot (by the week, the month or the quarter). A photograph from the phone or the document register carries its station and its place; one that names no station is given the one station within 30 m of where it was taken, and otherwise waits as "place not set" for a person. A station's shots are read side by side, this period beside the last or any two picked, and as at a past day. The stations not photographed this period are one line under Needs you, as any period that is due is. The monthly progress report prints each station's shot of its month beside the month before, and the owner pack places them under Time.
- **A milestone and its photographs.** A station may be marked as needed before its milestone reads complete. Marking that milestone a hundred per cent while such a station has no shot this period is refused, with the stations named. A lead or signer of Construction may complete it all the same by picking why from a short list; no sentence is written. A milestone completed before this, or reported complete from site, reads "complete, photographs wanting" until the photographs are taken or the reason is picked.
- **One calendar.** A week, a month and a quarter mean the same thing for a cash month, a RERA quarter and a photograph round: Monday to Sunday, the calendar month, and the quarter numbered in the Indian financial year unless the project says the calendar year.
- **A first period, and a late one.** A set nobody has kept says "No period kept yet · Start with October 2026" and asks for nothing until it is started; a set with a certificate of each kind, or one for each building, opens a row for each. A new period opens with what holds already filled and marked as carried, and what is read afresh blank with last period's value beside it ("last: ₹4.1 Cr"); a worked-out figure whose input is blank says which field it waits for. A period issued after it ended is written down as at its last day, not the day of issue: September issued on 13 October carries nothing recorded in October. Whoever issues a set is told once when a period falls due, whether or not a report day is set.
- **Names.** Wherever a line says who typed, accepted or issued something, it gives the name the project's team gives them, never an address.

The honest limits. History starts with this release: a field's past begins at its first change after it, and a period issued for a month before that freezes those figures as they read on the day of issue, marked "as now, no history before" the day and listed on the issued sheet. Figures from the cost register and the tenders keep no past at all, and print "as now" in any report or issue for a past month. The cut for a late issue is by when a thing was recorded, so a September payment typed in October is not in September as issued; the sheet's own entries are, whenever typed. A report from the phone that a milestone is complete is taken as reported and is not refused for want of a photograph: the milestone then reads "complete, photographs wanting". The phone app's station capture (the list of stations due, last period's shot as a guide, the station carried on the photograph) is typechecked and has not yet been run on a device. A model reads nothing of a field's past.

## Figures at each level

Three sets of figures, each a count that opens the rows behind it, every row naming its source record and opening it on its project. **Portfolio**, "Across projects": forecast against budget for each project and each currency from the last issued Cost Report, with the movement since the one before; planned against projected completion and extension-of-time flags; papers owed by party; open alerts by kind; what waits on the owner. **A project's Overview**, "By department": values and cards waiting for acceptance with the oldest's age, actions open and overdue, requests out with their age. **My work**, "By person": open, overdue, accepted in the last 30 days, and the median days a thing waited. Money is read from issued Cost Reports only; a draft's figures are not counted, and two currencies are never added together. A person reads their own figures, and a lead of a department on a project reads a colleague's for that project; a viewer and a collaborator read nobody's, and nothing ranks or scores people. They are counts and lists, with no chart. Limits: acceptances made from a card on a check are not counted; papers owed adds checklist lines and the documents checks expect without removing what is in both; open alerts are as fresh as the last alert run.

## The firm's menu, and Set up

The sidebar lists the firm's work first (Portfolio, My work, Requests, People) and, under a rule, reference and how the workspace is set up (Law and policy, Libraries, Set up, Integrations, About). An entry is listed only for somebody who can use it: Law and policy and Set up for the firm's own people, Integrations for its owner and managers. A direct visit by anybody else says whose page it is and asks nothing of the server. The list and who is shown each entry are one function, held by a test.

**My work** opens with Notices (what was filed, accepted or answered that concerns the person, each with the one thing to do), then the work with their name on it, late first, then their own figures. A notice that a request or a paper owed was answered opens the Documents of the department it belongs to, where a reply is confirmed on its line; one about a request that belongs to no department opens Requests.

**Requests**, on the sidebar, is the same requests across every project: replies to confirm first, then the To sort tray, then what is overdue, what is asked for, drafts and what closed recently.

**Set up** lists each thing that can be connected or scheduled, with one line saying how it stands: working, not set up (with what is needed, in a sentence a partner can act on or forward, and the exact setting for whoever runs the deployment), failing (with the last error), or not used yet. For the firm: email to its people, requests by email, requests by WhatsApp, Google Drive, the timer, the AI model, the law and policy sources, and, for an owner or manager, API keys and webhooks. For each project: its Drive folder and its report day. A row links to where it is changed: Law and policy, Integrations, the project's Documents, the project's Reports. Nothing is changed on Set up itself, and other pages that used to name a setting now point here. It is read by the firm's own people; somebody working from a grant is refused.

A native app for Android and iOS, for site work only: the daily site log (manpower, work done, progress against milestones, weather), geo-tagged photos, issues and snags. It works with no signal and syncs when it can. It signs in by scanning a pairing code from the web app.

## Integrations

Read-only public lookups: the state's published land map by survey number, published guidance values, map layers (water, drains, planning zones) and places. Portal listings for comparables are searched where a scraping service is configured. Land records, encumbrance certificates and RERA registration are not fetched: the registries have no interface a machine may use, so the app names the portal and how to get the paper, and reads the paper once it is filed.

Out of the app go files: PDF, Word, Excel and CSV. Nothing is written to government portals or banks.

**A Google Drive folder.** A project can be connected to one Google Drive folder, where the deployment has a Google service account (`GOOGLE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT_JSON`); without one the screen says Google Drive is not set up and points to Set up. Its owner shares the folder with the service account as a viewer and pastes the link on Documents › Connect a folder. Files new or changed in the folder, and in folders inside it, are taken in the way a dropped paper is: stored, read here, and put on the record with what they state waiting for a person. Each file says the folder path it came from. A folder a project of another firm is connected to is refused. Somebody working from a grant sees the paper but not its folder path or link. A Google Doc, Slides file or Drawing is taken as a PDF and a Sheet as xlsx. A file over 25 MB, an empty file, a copy of a file already taken, and a kind that cannot be read (video, audio, a Google Form, a shortcut) are listed as left out with the reason. The folder is read every hour by a timer, 25 files a project a run, and at once by "Sync now" (10 files and a minute at a time). No model is asked by the sync; "Read the filed documents" in the chat sends those papers to a model as it does any other. Everything is filed as a paper: questionnaires, site photographs and meeting notes are not sorted as a drop in the chat sorts them. The hourly read does not see a file moved into the folder from elsewhere in Drive; "Sync now" does. It has not been run against Google's live service. OneDrive is not built.

**Requests by email and WhatsApp.** A request can be sent to an outside party by email (Resend) or WhatsApp (Meta Cloud API), each where its settings are given; a channel with a setting missing sends nothing and says so. Their reply, with its papers, comes back onto the request as "answered, waiting for a person to confirm"; nothing is marked received until one of the firm's people confirms it. Mail that names no open request, or comes from anywhere but the address or number the request was sent to or one of the firm's own members, or by email from a sender whose domain did not pass DMARC, waits in a To sort tray on the Requests page until a person files it to a project or discards it. A party whose domain publishes no DMARC record therefore never answers straight onto a request. The tray holds 500 items for a firm and 20 from one sender; a stranger who names no request has their words kept and their attachments named, not stored, and a message past the sender's 20 is dropped, not retried. A request holds five replies waiting, and a sixth goes to the tray. What a reply's papers state counts for nothing until a person confirms the reply. Neither channel has been tried against the live services. A voice note, video, sticker or location on WhatsApp is acknowledged and ignored, and an email's HTML body is never read, only its text. Confirming a reply to a request that covers several checklist lines closes the request and marks no line.

**For other systems.** A firm's owner or manager makes read-only API keys on the Integrations page, which the sidebar lists for them alone, (projects, one project's summary, alerts, issued reports, papers owed) and registers https addresses to be sent signed webhooks when a paper is filed, a value is accepted, an alert is raised or resolved, or a report is issued. Webhooks need `CREDENTIAL_KEY` set; keys work without it. Nothing is written through the API. A delivery that fails is tried again up to five times, then the endpoint is marked failing and events for it are dropped until a Test delivery goes through. The detail is in [integrations.md](integrations.md).

There is no connector to OneDrive or to a mailbox as such, and no MCP server for another system; see the table below.
